The Government announced a package of RSE scheme changes on 28 July 2026. Nothing changes for employers or workers now. The rules stage in from early 2027 and run through 2029, and they reshape three things accredited seasonal employers care about: how long accreditation lasts, how freely workers move between employers, and which costs an employer may recover from a worker.[^1] [^2]

## Why this matters now

One decision lands well before the staged rollout starts. Accommodation standards are out for consultation with Pacific partners and employers, and the Minister says she expects "to take decisions by the end of September" — that is, September 2026, eight weeks from the announcement.[^2] Internet access is already named as an addition to the prescribed standards.[^2] Separately, the 2026/27 season cap has not been published as at the date of this note, so seasonal planning is still working from an unannounced number.

For employers who hold AEWV accreditation as well, there is a second reason to read this. Core horticulture and viticulture labour cannot be hired on an AEWV at all: immigration instructions state the proposed employment must not be for "planting, maintaining, harvesting, or packing crops in the horticulture or viticulture industries", and direct those applications to the RSE or Supplementary Seasonal Employment instructions instead.[^3] Whatever happens to RSE is therefore not an adjacent scheme problem. It is the only channel for that work. The [horticulture visa channels note]({{< ref "/blog/aewv-horticulture-seasonal-visas" >}}) sets out the full boundary.

## Accreditation becomes graduated, and the range is wide

The headline structural change is that RSE accreditation length will vary with compliance history rather than being uniform.

INZ states the model directly: "New employers will initially receive 1-year accreditation before becoming eligible for longer accreditation periods of 3 or 6 years, depending on their compliance history and track record."[^1] Immigration Minister Erica Stanford framed the package as a simplification: "over time the scheme has become more complicated and, in some areas, unnecessarily burdensome. We are making practical changes to simplify requirements, improve clarity and transparency, and strengthen protections for workers."[^2]

A six-year accreditation is a long way from anything in the AEWV regime, where renewals for standard and high-volume employers run 24 months. The design direction is the same in both schemes — a clean record buys administrative relief — but the RSE version pushes it much further.

The practical implication for a grower is that compliance history stops being only a risk to manage and becomes an asset with a measurable value: potentially five extra years between applications.

## Worker mobility and the Agreement to Recruit both loosen

Two process changes sit alongside the accreditation model.

Workers will have more flexibility to move between RSE employers "in appropriate circumstances with additional safeguard for workers."[^2] The announcement does not define the circumstances or the safeguards, and both are the sort of detail that arrives with the instructions rather than the press release.

On recruitment, the Agreement to Recruit process is being differentiated: "Employers endorsed by MSD will not need to show they have advertised roles or listed vacancies with MSD, while other employers will need to show us that they have listed their vacancies with MSD."[^1] That is a labour-market-test step removed for one group and retained for another — again on the basis of standing rather than the individual vacancy.

Work scope also widens, with "incidental tasks that support their primary role, such as operating machinery where appropriate."[^1]

## Cost recovery gets a standard agreement

The change with the most direct compliance consequence is the least eye-catching. INZ describes "a standard cost recovery agreement so workers can understand and agree to any costs before travelling to New Zealand."[^1] The ministerial release names the scope: "clearer rules on the genuine costs employers can recover from workers (such as transport, insurance and accommodation), giving workers and employers greater certainty about what costs can be recovered and how."[^2]

This is worth flagging to any employer who runs both schemes, because the two regimes are being pointed in opposite directions. RSE is moving towards a defined set of costs an employer may genuinely recover from a worker, agreed before travel.[^1] [^2] The AEWV allows no such thing — accreditation instructions bar passing recruitment, training or equipment costs to a visa holder or applicant in any form.[^4] An operator applying RSE cost-recovery habits to an AEWV worker is applying the wrong rulebook. Our [bonding clauses note]({{< ref "/blog/aewv-bonding-clauses-employment-agreement" >}}) covers the AEWV side of that line.

{{< callout type="info" >}}
**Our take.** The through-line in this package is the same one running through AEWV policy: differentiate employers by compliance history, then hand the good ones less process. That is defensible, and for a grower with clean records it is straightforwardly good news. It also raises the cost of a compliance stumble, because what you lose is no longer just a finding — it is the standing that shortens your paperwork for the next six years. Employers who have treated record-keeping as an overhead may want to re-price it before the graduated model starts.
{{< /callout >}}

## What to do

1. If you hold RSE status, put the accommodation standards consultation in your calendar. Decisions are expected by the end of September 2026, and internet access at accommodation is already named as a prescribed standard.[^2]
2. Audit your accommodation against an internet-access requirement now, while the cost can be planned rather than absorbed at short notice.
3. Assemble the compliance record you would submit for a longer accreditation. Under a graduated model, the evidence you can produce determines which band you land in.
4. Separate your RSE and AEWV cost-recovery documentation so no template crosses between them. What is recoverable under one scheme is barred under the other.[^4]
5. If you hire both seasonally and year-round, confirm which of your roles are barred from the AEWV entirely before next season's planning, not during it.[^3]
6. Watch for the 2026/27 cap announcement. It has not been published as at this writing, and it sets the ceiling everything else is planned against.

## What we don't know

Almost all of the operational detail. The announcement describes direction and timing — stages from early 2027 through 2029 — but the instructions that will actually bind employers have not been published.[^1] [^2] The circumstances in which a worker may move between employers, the safeguards attached, the criteria separating a three-year from a six-year accreditation, and the contents of the standard cost recovery agreement are all unstated.

The 2026/27 cap number is also not published as at the date of this note. And we have seen reporting of a further change extending migrant exploitation protection visas to RSE workers; it does not appear in either the INZ announcement or the ministerial release we have relied on here, so we are not treating it as confirmed.
