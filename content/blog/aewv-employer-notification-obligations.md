Holding AEWV accreditation creates four ongoing obligations to inform or involve Immigration New Zealand when things change — separate from, and additional to, your employment law obligations and the compliance conditions that came with accreditation.[^1] One runs on an explicit 10-working-day clock. One has no fixed timeframe. One requires a new Job Check before the change can take effect. All of them can result in accreditation revocation or suspension if missed, regardless of whether your underlying compliance is otherwise sound.[^1]

## Why this matters now

Notification failures are procedural breaches, not substantive ones. An employer who has been fully compliant with wage obligations, record-keeping, settlement information delivery, and genuine-efforts requirements can still face accreditation consequences for missing a notification. INZ's guidance is direct: failing to notify "means you will not be meeting your accreditation requirements."[^1] The notification record and the compliance record are separate in INZ's assessment.

This is worth being explicit about because most AEWV compliance checklists focus on the substantive obligations — wages, records, settlement, advertising. The notification obligations are a parallel track that is easy to overlook because they do not appear in post-accreditation check evidence lists as a standalone category.

## The four obligations

### 1. Worker departure — notify when it happens

When an AEWV worker stops working for you, you must notify INZ.[^1] The timing and mechanics are covered in detail in the [10-working-day notification article]({{< ref "/blog/aewv-worker-leaves-10-day-notification" >}}) — including the clock that runs from the date the employment relationship is recorded as ending (not the worker's last day), the less-than-one-month exemption that may apply in some cases, and what the notification must contain.

The key operational point for this article is that the departure notification sits outside normal HR offboarding. Final pay, resignation acceptance, and payroll exit processing are all separate events from the INZ notification — and each has a different owner in most organisations.

### 2. Key personnel changes — 10 working days

If the key people in your organisation change, you must notify INZ within 10 working days of that change.[^1] In the accreditation context, "key people" refers to the personnel whose details were provided as part of the accreditation application — typically directors, shareholders, and the responsible manager named on the application.

This clock runs regardless of the reason for the change. A director retiring, a shareholding transferring, or the named responsible manager moving to a different role — all trigger the 10-working-day obligation. Unlike worker departure, there is no published exemption for short-duration or temporary changes.

The practical risk is that a governance change or board appointment is handled through company-law and HR processes without anyone checking the AEWV accreditation implications. The trigger is the change itself, not any subsequent INZ review.

### 3. Business restructuring and insolvency — no fixed clock

Restructures, significant redundancy rounds, insolvency proceedings, and liquidation all require notification to INZ.[^1] The timeframe is not a specific day count: INZ states "contact us as early as possible."[^1]

That open-ended framing does not reduce the obligation — it means the obligation runs from when the event is foreseeable, not when it is concluded. An employer planning a redundancy round that will affect AEWV workers should notify INZ when the decision is confirmed, not when the workers have been told, and not after the process is complete.

The [business restructure and sale article]({{< ref "/blog/accredited-business-restructure-sale-migrant-workers" >}}) covers the full picture of what a restructure or sale means for the AEWV workforce, including the Job Change transfer process if the business is acquired.

### 4. Significant role or location changes — a new Job Check, not just a notification

If the details of a job change significantly after the original Job Check was approved — for example, the main duties of the role or the work location — INZ requires a new Job Check application.[^1] This is more involved than a written notification: it means going back through part of the accreditation process before the change takes effect.

The risk in many operations is that a role change or site reallocation is handled as an internal HR matter — a contract variation or a roster change — without anyone checking whether it triggers a new Job Check. A worker placed in a materially different role or at a different site without a new Job Check may be working outside their visa conditions.

This intersects with the [different-site infringement article]({{< ref "/blog/different-site-infringement-aewv" >}}), which covers the $3,000 body-corporate infringement that applies when a worker is found at a location not on their visa. The Job Check obligation is the earlier action — applying for the right to make the change before the change occurs.

## What the silence costs

INZ is clear on the consequence: missing a notification means you are not meeting your accreditation requirements, and accreditation can be revoked or suspended.[^1] Beyond the accreditation consequences, the worker whose role or location change was not notified — or whose status was not reported after a departure — may face visa complications. INZ's guidance notes that a worker in an undisclosed changed situation faces potential deportation liability.[^1]

The infringement scheme that runs alongside AEWV accreditation means that in some circumstances, an employer-side notification failure that results in a worker breaching their visa conditions can attract an infringement notice for the employer, separate from any accreditation action.

## What to do

1. Map each triggering event to a named person who owns the INZ step. Worker departure must not sit solely in standard HR offboarding. Key personnel changes must not sit solely in company-secretarial governance — the 10-working-day clock for key personnel starts from the date of the change, not from any internal process completion.
2. Add an AEWV notification check to your governance calendar for any board, director, or significant shareholder change. The 10-working-day window from the change date is short.
3. For restructures: notify INZ when the decision to restructure is confirmed and AEWV workers are in scope — not when the redundancy letters go out, and not when the process concludes.
4. Before approving any material change to an AEWV worker's role or work site, check whether the change requires a Job Change process through INZ rather than a simple internal reallocation. If it does, the notification happens as part of that process. If it does not formally require a Job Change but is still material, notify INZ directly.
5. Keep a log of each notification made — date, method used, and any INZ reference or confirmation received — alongside the accreditation record. If INZ later questions whether a notification was made, this is the evidence.

## What we don't know

INZ does not define "key people" with precision on the public employer guidance page, and the Operational Manual's WA2 chapter may contain more specific definitions — see the [Operational Manual guide]({{< ref "/blog/aewv-operational-manual-wa-chapter" >}}) for how to find and read WA2.

The "as early as possible" standard for restructuring has no published bright line. We do not know how INZ treats a notification made after the affected workers have been informed, or after a statutory insolvency process has begun. If timing is at all ambiguous in a restructure situation, engage a licensed adviser before the event, not after.

Whether a role change reaches the threshold of "significant" for Job Check purposes is not defined further on the public page. The safe assumption is that any change in main duties or work location that would have appeared in the original Job Check application should trigger a check as to whether a new Job Check is required before the change is made.
