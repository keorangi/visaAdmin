AEWV accreditation renewal evidence is not a re-run of your first application. The binding instruction tells an immigration officer to assess whether you have complied with a defined list of requirements "since their previous accreditation was granted."[^1] Renewal is a look-back over the period you have just finished. The documents that matter are the ones you generated while accredited — not the ones you assembled to become accredited.

## Why this matters now

INZ's public renewal page asks you to upload documents showing how you met the requirements and "the commitments you made during your initial application."[^2] That phrasing is easy to read as an administrative refresh. The Operational Manual is more direct about the consequence: where an employer has not complied since the last grant, the application "must be declined" unless three rectification tests are met.[^1] With almost 30,000 accredited employers now in the system as at 30 June 2026,[^3] most renewals are second or subsequent applications — which means most renewals are now look-backs rather than first assessments.

## The six things an officer must check

WA2.30 names them. In assessing a subsequent application, an officer must assess compliance since the previous grant with:[^1]

1. **Settlement support activities** (WA2.10.5).
2. **Specific immigration standards** (WA2.10.10(c) to (f)).
3. **Business standards** (WA2.10.10(m) to (p)).
4. **Triangular placement requirements**, if you place AEWV workers with controlling third parties.
5. **Reasonable steps** to ensure an applicant meets qualification and skill requirements (WA2.10.14).
6. **The duty to advise INZ of a change in circumstances** (WA2.35(a) and (b)).

Where compliance has failed, the escape route is narrow. The employer must have rectified the non-compliance promptly, taken sufficient steps to address its cause, and satisfied INZ they will comply in future periods.[^1] The officer may weigh the seriousness and the number of instances. They may also weigh whether the employer complied in previous accreditation periods, and "evidence that the employer has put in place appropriate measures to prevent similar non-compliance in future, such as processes and training."[^1]

That last factor is the one you can still act on. A documented process change dated close to the incident is evidence. An explanation written the week you renew is not.

The manual closes the point without softening it: "The onus is on the employer to satisfy INZ."[^1]

## Financial evidence — four routes, and an exemption list

To show the business is viable and genuinely operating, evidence must establish one of four alternatives. The business has:[^4]

- not made a loss, before depreciation and tax, in the last two years; or
- had positive cash flow in each of the last six months; or
- sufficient capital or external investment — from a founder, parent company, trust or another source — to remain viable and ongoing; or
- a credible business plan for at least the next two years showing how it will remain viable, for example by holding contracts for work.

Each route has its own document set. For the no-loss route, INZ names the latest available IR tax return documents — an IR4 companies income tax return and an IR10 financial statement summary — or annual reports or financial statements for the past two years.[^4] Where a period is not covered by previous financial reports, an interim financial report can fill the gap.[^4] For the cash-flow route, INZ asks for a current six-month cashflow statement broken down by month, alongside two years of annual reports or financial statements.[^4] For the capital route, add bank statements showing the available balance and financial forecasts or cashflow projections.[^4] Where the latest financial year's accounts are not ready, a draft copy is accepted.[^4]

Some organisations do not need to provide this evidence at all: government departments, foreign governments and missions, crown entities and state-owned enterprises, local authorities, state and state-integrated schools, and NZX-listed companies with at least 100 employees.[^4]

## Settlement support — nine items, and a declaration that costs you

Settlement support is the requirement most likely to be evidenced thinly, because it is delivered once per worker and rarely filed anywhere central.

The Operational Manual lists nine things that must be provided:[^5]

- accommodation options
- transport options, including driving and driver licence information, and public transport
- the cost of living
- how to access healthcare services
- Citizens Advice Bureau services
- relevant community groups
- how to obtain an IRD number from Inland Revenue
- industry training and qualification information and options
- specific job or industry hazards

It must be completed within one month of the worker beginning employment as an AEWV holder.[^5] It does not need repeating for the same worker if the information first provided has not changed.[^5] Acceptable evidence includes copies of the settlement information, the communications sending it to the worker, and onboarding or induction policies, guidelines or plans.[^5]

The declaration is where this becomes visible at renewal. If you declare that you have not given settlement information to all AEWV workers recruited since your last accreditation application within one month of them starting work, INZ asks for supporting evidence.[^2] That means an explanation, copies of policies implemented so it does not recur, and documents showing steps taken to ensure the information is provided in future.[^2] The request maps directly onto the WA2.30 rectification test. Answering it well is not a formality.

One point of currency worth noting: the separate requirement for employers and workers to complete Employment New Zealand's online modules was removed on 27 January 2025 and does not apply to current or future accredited employers.[^6] Employers are still expected to provide employment and settlement information.[^6]

## The notification record that can decline a renewal on its own

Employers must tell INZ within 10 working days of any change to key persons or business structure.[^7] That includes a change to the legal entity itself — moving from a partnership to a limited company, or merging or amalgamating with another entity.[^7] The same 10-working-day clock applies when a regular AEWV or Peak Seasonal Visa holder's employment ends more than a month before their visa expires.[^7]

The manual then states the consequence directly: "An employer accreditation renewal application may be declined if an employer has failed to advise INZ of any changes outlined in (a) or (b) above within the required timeframe."[^7]

This is the item most likely to be discovered late, because the failure happened months earlier and produced no signal at the time. A worker who resigned 14 months ago with six weeks left on their visa is a notification event. If nobody told INZ inside 10 working days, that sits in the renewal assessment now.

*AEWV Compliance is scoping a tool in this area — tracking notification obligations against their clocks is one of the problems we are looking at.*

## What to do

1. Build a single dated log covering the period since your last accreditation was granted: key-person changes, legal-entity or structure changes, and every AEWV worker whose employment ended more than a month before their visa expiry. Record the date INZ was told, not just the date it happened.
2. For any notification that was late or missed, write the rectification record now — what changed in your process, when, and who owns it. WA2.30 weighs measures taken to prevent recurrence.[^1]
3. Choose your financial evidence route before you start assembling documents. The four routes need different packs, and mixing them produces a thicker application, not a stronger one.
4. Pull settlement support evidence per worker recruited since the last grant, and check it against the nine required items rather than against your own onboarding pack. Missing hazard information or IRD guidance is a common gap.
5. If you place workers with controlling third parties, add the placement, monitoring and issue-resolution records — these are separately assessed at renewal under WA2.30(a)(iv).[^1]
6. Upload the optional evidence INZ names — GST or PAYE records showing your IRD number, and a current organisation chart without employee names — even though it is optional. INZ states it processes applications faster with them.[^2]

## What we don't know

INZ does not publish how often renewals are declined, or the distribution of reasons. We know 1,493 employers have had accreditation revoked and 961 suspended as at 30 June 2026,[^3] but revocation and suspension are separate processes from a declined renewal, and INZ notes a number of revocations are due to liquidation.[^3] Nothing published lets you infer a renewal decline rate from those figures.

It is also unclear how far back "since their previous accreditation was granted" reaches for an employer on a 24-month cycle who has had an intervening upgrade or type change. The instruction refers to the previous grant, but does not address which grant governs where several have occurred.
