A horticulture employer hiring migrant workers in 2026 is choosing between five visa channels under three different accreditation regimes: the regular Accredited Employer Work Visa, two seasonal AEWV variants, the Recognised Seasonal Employer scheme, and Supplementary Seasonal Employer approval.[^1] The AEWV horticulture picture changed twice in the past year — the Specific Purpose Work Visa closed as a seasonal option on 8 December 2025, and four horticulture leading-hand occupations joined the National Occupation List at skill level 1.[^1][^2] The compliance failures in this sector are mostly channel failures: the right worker hired through the wrong visa, or hired outside the months the visa allows.

## Why this matters now

Winter is the horticulture sector's planning window. Most of the seasonal occupations INZ recognises for the seasonal AEWVs have seasons starting in October or November.[^3] An employer who wants workers in the ground by spring needs the channel question settled now — including whether their existing accreditation type covers the visa they intend to use, and whether a Job Check with advertising or Work and Income engagement sits in the critical path.

## Five seasonal visa channels, three accreditation regimes

INZ publishes a side-by-side comparison of the five seasonal hiring options.[^1] The structural point is that they split across three accreditation regimes:

| Visa | Accreditation needed | Maximum duration |
|------|---------------------|------------------|
| Accredited Employer Work Visa (AEWV) | AEWV accreditation + Job Check | 5 years most roles; 3 years for ANZSCO level 4–5 |
| Global Workforce Seasonal Visa (GWSV) | AEWV accreditation + Job Check | 3 years, max 9 months in NZ per year |
| Peak Seasonal Visa (PSV) | AEWV accreditation + Job Check | Up to 7 months per year |
| Recognised Seasonal Employer (RSE) Limited Visa | RSE accreditation | Up to 7 months in any 11 (9 for Tuvalu and Kiribati citizens) |
| Supplementary Seasonal Employer (SSE) Visa | SSE Approval in Principle | Up to 6 months |

AEWV accreditation covers the first three. It does not entitle an employer to hire RSE workers, and RSE accreditation does not cover the AEWV family. An operation using both — common at scale in horticulture — is running two accreditation regimes with separate obligations, renewals, and audit exposure.

Until late 2025 there was a sixth channel: seasonal hiring on a Specific Purpose Work Visa. That closed on 8 December 2025 — INZ now points seasonal employers to the PSV and GWSV instead.[^1] An employer whose seasonal plan still assumes SPWV hires is a season out of date.

## What stays RSE and SSE territory

The RSE Limited Visa covers workers from eligible Pacific nations in planting, maintaining, harvesting, and packing crops in horticulture and viticulture.[^1] The SSE visa covers people already in New Zealand on student or visitor visas doing the same categories of seasonal work.[^1] Neither is an AEWV, and the AEWV seasonal variants do not reach into that core picking-and-packing work: the GWSV and PSV job lists cover occupations like winery cellar hands, mussel and oyster farm workers, forestry workers, and shearers — not general harvest labour.[^3]

The boundary matters because it is easy to misread the seasonal AEWVs as an alternative to RSE accreditation. On INZ's published job lists, they are not. A grower who needs harvest crews and holds only AEWV accreditation has a structural gap, not a paperwork one.

## The seasonal AEWVs — GWSV and PSV mechanics

The two seasonal AEWV variants are built for different workforces.[^1]

**GWSV — the returning skilled seasonal worker.** Three-year, multi-season visa for workers with at least three seasons' experience in the past six years. The worker can spend a maximum of nine months per year in New Zealand and must spend three months in a row outside New Zealand each year of the visa.[^4] A Job Check is required, but the employer is exempt from providing proof of advertising.[^1]

**PSV — the short peak.** Up to seven months, for workers with at least one season in the past three years. After reaching the maximum stay, the worker must spend four months in a row outside New Zealand before another PSV can be granted.[^4] The Job Check requires advertising and engagement with Work and Income — or a Peak Seasonal Visa endorsement.[^1] The [MSD engagement article]({{< ref "/blog/aewv-msd-engagement-labour-market-test" >}}) covers what that engagement record needs to show.

Neither visa carries an English language requirement — unlike the regular AEWV, where skill level 3 to 5 roles need English evidence from 1 June 2026.[^1]

Two constraints apply to both. First, INZ publishes recognised season months for each eligible occupation — a wine maker's season runs January to June, a forestry worker's April to September — and while shoulder-month variations are acknowledged, you cannot employ workers outside the conditions of their visa: nine months a year for GWSV holders, seven for PSV.[^3] Second, seasonal AEWV workers can only work in the regions or locations listed on their visa; moving them to a different site needs a Job Change.[^1] The [different-site infringement article]({{< ref "/blog/different-site-infringement-aewv" >}}) covers what happens when that rule is missed.

## The year-round roles that moved into the regular AEWV

Not every horticulture role is seasonal. From 3 November 2025, the National Occupation List recognises four horticulture leading-hand occupations for the regular AEWV at skill level 1: Horticulture Post-Harvest Leading Hand, Fruit Production Leading Hand, Indoor Crop Production Leading Hand, and Outdoor Crop Leading Hand.[^2] From 9 March 2026, Nursery Production Leading Hand sits at skill level 2 and Advanced Agricultural and Horticultural Mobile Plant Operator at skill level 3.[^2]

Skill level 1 and 2 classification carries real consequences: eligibility for the five-year maximum continuous stay, and no English language requirement.[^4][^5] For supervisory and post-harvest roles that horticulture employers previously squeezed into lower-skilled ANZSCO codes — with three-year stay caps — the NOL codes may change the retention arithmetic. How the stay clock runs, and what happens when it expires, is covered in the [maximum continuous stay article]({{< ref "/blog/aewv-maximum-continuous-stay" >}}).

One detail worth care: the NOL's Advanced Agricultural and Horticultural Mobile Plant Operator (code 721115, regular AEWV) sits one digit away from the GWSV's seasonal Agricultural and Horticultural Mobile Plant Operator (ANZSCO 721111).[^2][^3] Near-identical titles, different visas, different rules. Classification by job title alone is how channel errors start.

## What to do

1. List every migrant worker on site and the visa each holds — AEWV, GWSV, PSV, RSE, or SSE. If the list takes more than a day to produce, that is the first gap to fix.
2. Match your accreditation types against next season's hiring plan. AEWV accreditation does not cover RSE hires, and RSE accreditation does not cover the seasonal AEWVs.[^1]
3. For GWSV and PSV workers, diarise the in-country limits — nine months per year and seven months respectively — and the required time outside New Zealand, per worker, from their actual arrival dates.[^4]
4. Check any role you plan to classify under the new NOL horticulture occupations against the published NOL codes before the next Job Check, and note the skill level consequences for stay length and English evidence.[^2]
5. If your seasonal plan still references Specific Purpose Work Visa hires, rebuild it — that channel closed on 8 December 2025.[^1]

## What we don't know

INZ's season tables are guidelines, and the shoulder-month language leaves the edges soft — how far a season can stretch before a hire is "outside the conditions of their visa" is not defined beyond the hard nine- and seven-month caps.[^3] We also do not know whether the GWSV and PSV eligible-occupation lists will expand into core horticulture harvest work, or whether that boundary with the RSE scheme is deliberate policy that will hold. Treat the current lists as current, not permanent.
