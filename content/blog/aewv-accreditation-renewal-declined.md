There is no statutory right of appeal against a declined AEWV employer accreditation application.[^1] What exists instead is a reconsideration, and it must be requested within 14 days.[^1] [^2] The reconsideration is decided on the information you already filed — an immigration officer "is not obligated to consider new information" provided with the request.[^1] That single sentence determines whether reconsideration or a fresh application is the right move, and the choice has to be made inside two weeks.

## Why this matters now

A declined renewal is not a rare administrative event with a soft landing. INZ names three grounds that apply to an accreditation application: it was missing key information, false or misleading information was provided or important information withheld, or the requirements were not met.[^2] A fourth ground — that accreditation has expired, or been suspended or revoked — is stated for job check applications specifically.[^2] As at 30 June 2026, 1,493 employers have had accreditation revoked and 961 have had it suspended, out of almost 30,000 accredited employers.[^3] INZ notes a number of revocations follow liquidation,[^3] so those figures are not a decline rate — but they show the enforcement end of the scheme is active, and a renewal is where a compliance history surfaces.

## Decline, suspension and revocation are three different things

They are often used interchangeably and they have different triggers and different consequences.

**A decline** ends an application. Your existing accreditation, if still current, is unaffected by the decline itself — but it continues to run toward its own expiry date.

**A suspension** pauses an accreditation you hold. An officer may suspend for up to three months at a time, or until verification, compliance or investigation activity concludes, whichever comes first.[^4] Where a prosecution is underway for specified non-compliance, the officer **must** suspend, and it remains in place until the prosecution concludes.[^4] Any suspension must be approved by an Immigration Manager.[^4] While suspended, INZ may decline to decide a Job Check application until the suspension ends.[^5]

**A revocation** ends an accreditation. An officer will revoke where verification determines the employer does not meet the requirements; where the employer refuses INZ entry for a site visit without reasonable justification and the officer is therefore not satisfied; or where the employer does not provide requested information within 10 working days without reasonable justification.[^4] Revocation must be approved by an INZ Head of Operations.[^4] Unused job tokens are cancelled.[^5]

One structural rule sits outside all three. Accreditation "remains with the legal entity that was granted accreditation," and where an accredited employer merges or amalgamates with a non-accredited employer, accreditation is "deemed to be revoked" if the final entity is a new entity or the existing non-accredited one.[^4] No decision is required for that to happen. It follows from the transaction.

## The 14-day reconsideration window

The Operational Manual sets it out: INZ may reconsider a declined employer accreditation application "where the reconsideration request is made within 14 calendar days of the date of decision on the application."[^1] INZ's public guidance describes the same window as 14 days "starting from the day after your application was declined."[^2] The two framings differ by a day at the margin. Neither gives you room to treat this as a fortnight of thinking time.

The window is enforced in the system rather than by discretion: once it has ended, the "Request reconsideration" link no longer displays on the application summary page in Immigration Online.[^2]

Three further features of the process:

- The application is reconsidered under the instructions that applied to the original application, not the current ones.[^1]
- It is reconsidered by a different immigration officer from the one who declined it.[^1] [^2]
- A fee is payable — NZD $250 for an accreditation reconsideration, and the same for a job check reconsideration.[^6]

## Reconsideration is decided on the file you already filed

This is the part that decides the strategy, and INZ states it in both places.

The manual: an officer "is not obligated to consider new information that is provided with a request for reconsideration, or a change in circumstances that occurred after the decision."[^1] In deciding whether to look at it, the officer "should consider whether these would be better considered as part of a new application."[^1]

The public guidance is blunter: "We reconsider applications based on the information you provided when you applied. If you provide new information, then you should submit a new application."[^2] And it names when reconsideration is unlikely to work — where the application was declined because it was missing information, or because you did not meet the requirements.[^2]

The rule that follows is simple. Reconsideration is for arguing that the assessment of what you filed was wrong. It is not a second chance to file better. INZ asks you to "explain what parts of our decision you think were incorrectly assessed and why."[^2] If your honest answer is that you should have sent more, a new application is the faster route — it costs $775 for standard accreditation against $250 for a reconsideration that is unlikely to succeed,[^6] but it is the one that can actually change the outcome.

One protection worth knowing about sits earlier in the process. Before declining on the basis of potentially prejudicial information, INZ gives employers "the opportunity to comment."[^7] If you receive such a letter, that is the point at which new information is genuinely in play — not after the decision.

## What happens to your workers

The workforce consequences follow the accreditation, not the application.

If your existing accreditation is still current, a declined renewal changes nothing immediately. If it expires with no renewal in force, workers already employed and in New Zealand can continue in their roles until their own visas expire, where their visa conditions permit employment with an accredited employer.[^8] What stops is your ability to apply for new Job Checks, to support a further AEWV, and to support residence applications that require an accredited employer.[^8]

If accreditation is suspended or revoked rather than expired, INZ contacts affected migrants who are not in New Zealand, as they may not be able to travel here on their AEWV.[^5] Workers already in the country can continue to work for you.[^5]

## What to do

1. Diary the 14-day clock the day a decline email arrives, dated from the decision. Do not wait for internal sign-off to start — the link disappears when the window closes.[^2]
2. Answer one question first: was the decision a misreading of what you sent, or was what you sent incomplete? Misreading points to reconsideration. Incomplete points to a new application.[^2]
3. Read the decline email against the specific requirement cited, not against your recollection of the application. INZ tells you why it declined.[^2]
4. If you receive a letter inviting comment on potentially prejudicial information, treat it as the substantive opportunity to put new material in front of INZ.[^7] It is the last stage at which new information is clearly considered.
5. Check whether any current accreditation is still running and when it expires. That date, not the decline, sets when Job Checks and further-visa support stop.[^8]
6. If a merger or amalgamation is in progress, confirm which legal entity and NZBN will employ your AEWV workers. Accreditation cannot be transferred to another legal entity, and cannot be acquired by a non-accredited entity merging with an accredited one.[^9]

## What we don't know

INZ does not publish how many accreditation renewals are declined, how many reconsiderations are requested, or how many succeed. Without that, we cannot say whether reconsideration is a realistic remedy in practice or a formality most employers skip in favour of reapplying.

The 14-day window is also described two ways — "within 14 calendar days of the date of decision" in the Operational Manual,[^1] and 14 days "starting from the day after your application was declined" in the public guidance.[^2] We have not found anything reconciling them. If you are near the edge of the window, assume the earlier reading.
