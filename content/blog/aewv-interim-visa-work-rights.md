When a worker's AEWV application is still undecided on the day their current visa expires, what happens next is not a judgement call — it is a rule with two branches. Workers applying from an AEWV, another work visa, or a student visa with term-time work rights get an Interim Visa carrying the work rights of the AEWV they applied for. Workers applying from any other visa get an Interim Visa with no work rights at all.[^1] Which branch your worker lands on determines whether the roster for next week is lawful.

## Why this matters now

Renewals carry more moving parts than they used to, which means more of them are still in process when the old visa runs out. For AEWVs applied for on or after 10 March 2025, every further AEWV needs a new approved Job Check, and since 1 June 2026 renewals in skill level 3 roles can require English language evidence.[^2] Each added step stretches the timeline between application and decision — and the Interim Visa is what fills the gap.

## How an interim visa works

An Interim Visa cannot be applied for. It is normally granted automatically when a temporary visa holder in New Zealand has applied for another temporary visa and the current one is about to run out. It starts the day after the current visa expires, and lets the person stay for up to 6 months while INZ decides the new application.[^3] Three endings matter as much as the start: if the new application is declined, the Interim Visa expires 21 days after the decline; if no decision has been made after 6 months, it expires anyway; and if the person leaves New Zealand, it expires immediately.[^3]

That last rule is easy to miss in practice. A worker who flies out for a family visit while on an Interim Visa does not come back to the same position they left.

## Work rights depend on the visa the worker held

For AEWV applicants, INZ's rule is specific. If the worker's expiring visa is a current AEWV, another work visa, or a student visa that allows work during term time, the Interim Visa carries "the same work rights as the AEWV you are applying for".[^1] If the worker is applying from any other visa — a visitor visa, or a student visa without term-time work rights — the Interim Visa does not allow work.[^1]

These are the settings in force since 8 April 2025, when INZ widened interim work rights for AEWV applicants to cover anyone applying from any type of work visa or an eligible student visa, letting them begin the specified job while the application is assessed.[^4]

Note the direction of the conditions: the interim work rights are those of the AEWV *applied for*, not the visa that expired. Where the application is for the same job with the same employer, nothing visible changes. Where the worker has applied to move — a different employer or a materially different role — the Interim Visa attaches to the new job. Continuing to roster the worker in the old arrangement once the interim period starts may no longer match their conditions, and employing someone inconsistently with a work-related visa condition is an infringement offence carrying a $1,000 fee for an individual employer or $3,000 for a body corporate, per employee.[^5] The [different-site infringement article]({{< ref "/blog/different-site-infringement-aewv" >}}) covers how narrow those condition mismatches can be.

## The clock still runs

An Interim Visa is not neutral time. For workers between AEWVs, time spent on an Interim Visa with work rights counts toward the maximum continuous stay — the total AEWV time before a worker must spend 12 months outside New Zealand.[^4] A worker who waits three months on an Interim Visa has spent three months of runway without holding an AEWV for any of it. The [maximum continuous stay article]({{< ref "/blog/aewv-maximum-continuous-stay" >}}) covers why that date is worth tracking per worker. The same interim time can also count toward work experience requirements for residence pathways — a point in the worker's favour worth recording accurately.[^4]

## What the employer's file should show

The Interim Visa is granted automatically, but nothing about the employer's position is automatic. VisaView, INZ's employer checking tool, shows only a person's current visa — its expiry and conditions — and does not show the status of applications INZ has not yet decided.[^6] So on the day a visa expires, the employer's evidence that the worker may keep working is the interim visa itself, not the pending application. A dated record — an eVisa copy or a VisaView enquiry made after the interim visa takes effect — is what shows the check was done at the moment it mattered.

*AEWV Compliance is scoping a tool in this area — a per-worker view that holds visa expiry, application status, and interim coverage together is one of the concepts we are testing.*

## What to do

1. For any worker with an AEWV application in process, diarise the current visa's expiry date and check their status on the day after — that is when the Interim Visa starts, and when your evidence should date from.[^3]
2. Before the expiry date, confirm which branch the worker is on: applying from an AEWV, work visa, or term-time-work student visa means interim work rights; applying from a visitor or non-work student visa means none.[^1]
3. If the application is for a different job or employer, treat the interim period as governed by the new job's conditions, not the old visa's — and take advice before continuing the old arrangement.[^1]
4. Tell workers on Interim Visas that leaving New Zealand ends the visa immediately — before they book travel, not after.[^3]
5. Add interim periods to your maximum continuous stay tracking: time on an Interim Visa with work rights counts toward the total.[^4] The [visa-expiry reminder records]({{< ref "/blog/aewv-visa-expiry-reminder-records" >}}) you already keep are the natural place for both dates.

## What we don't know

INZ's guidance says an Interim Visa expires after 6 months if no decision has been made — but what a worker's lawful status and work rights are from that point, while the application is still undecided, is not spelled out on the public pages. We also have not found published guidance on how quickly an Interim Visa becomes visible in VisaView once it takes effect, which matters for an employer relying on a day-one check. And whether INZ expects employers to hold specific evidence for the interim period, beyond the general record-keeping standard, is not stated. Where a worker's status is unclear at expiry, verify before the next shift rather than after.
