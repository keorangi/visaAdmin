The AEWV 30 hours a week rule is not a target you average towards. Immigration instructions require the employment to be "full time (at least 30 guaranteed hours per week for every week worked) for the duration of the employment period."[^1] Since 7 April 2024 it has also been a condition written onto the visa itself.[^2] A quiet fortnight that drops a worker to 24 hours is therefore not a payroll variance. INZ puts the consequence in one sentence: "If you do not provide at least 30 hours a week's employment we can revoke your accreditation."[^2]

## Why this matters now

Guaranteed hours are the easiest AEWV setting to breach by accident, because nothing in a normal business rhythm protects them. Rosters shrink in a downturn. A worker asks to go part-time. A site closes for a week. None of those events look like immigration decisions when they happen, and none of them trigger an immigration prompt. But INZ's post-accreditation check guide asks for "records of the hours worked, payment of wages and PAYE, and holidays and leave taken by your AEWV workers" — so the evidence is the timesheet, and the timesheet is what an officer reads.[^7]

## The 30-hour floor binds at two stages, not one

The rule appears first at Job Check. Instructions on acceptable employment state that the proposed employment must be for a single accredited employer only and "must be full time (at least 30 guaranteed hours per week for every week worked) for the duration of the employment period."[^1]

It then binds again when the visa is decided. The employment offered must match what was approved at Job Check on location, occupation, remuneration, direct employer, and hours — and on hours the instruction is directional: "the minimum guaranteed hours of work must not be less, and the maximum hours must not be more, than those approved as part of the Job Check application."[^4] The manual adds that the employment "must continue to meet all other requirements for acceptable employment as specified at WA3.15.5 and WA3.15.10, in effect at the time the Accredited Employer work visa application is made."[^4]

Two consequences follow. If you approved a Job Check at 40 guaranteed hours, the agreement cannot offer 32. And if you later want to move a worker to genuinely different hours, you are not editing a contract — you are changing something the visa was granted on.

## It is a visa condition, and that changes who is exposed

INZ was explicit when the condition was introduced: "From 7 April 2024, new AEWVs will be issued with a condition requiring you to employ migrants for at least 30 hours per week."[^2] That page is now more than two years old, but the requirement is carried by current instructions — WA3.15.10 as at 8 December 2025 and WA4.10.1 as at 9 March 2026 — so it is live, not historical.[^1] [^4]

The worker-facing page states the other half — an AEWV holder must "work in New Zealand for an accredited employer who has offered you at least 30 hours work a week," may "work only in the job, for the employer and in the location stated on your visa," and "cannot work a second job."[^3] A worker cut to 24 hours cannot make the shortfall up elsewhere. The second-job bar closes that door.

That is why the exposure sits with the employer. Accreditation instructions require that the employer and its key persons must not have "employed a person in a manner that is inconsistent with a work-related condition of that person's" visa.[^5] Hours are a condition of the visa, so a sustained shortfall sits squarely in that frame. Where verification finds an employer does not meet the accreditation requirements, an immigration officer "will revoke" accreditation, and a suspension of up to three months can run while a compliance case is open.[^6]

{{< callout type="info" >}}
**Our take.** The 30-hour rule is unusual among AEWV settings because breaching it can feel generous rather than exploitative. An employer who keeps someone on the books through a slow quarter at 22 hours a week, rather than ending the employment, has done the humane thing commercially and the non-compliant thing under immigration instructions. The instructions do not offer a carve-out for good intentions, and we have not found one anywhere in the published rules. That mismatch is worth knowing about before it is discovered in a check.
{{< /callout >}}

## Where the 30 hours gets confused with the other 30 hours

There are two separate 30-hour rules in the AEWV rulebook and they answer different questions.

The one covered here is a per-worker floor: every AEWV holder must be guaranteed at least 30 hours a week.[^1] The other applies only to employers placing workers with third parties, where at least 15% of the staff placed must be New Zealand citizens or residents guaranteed at least 30 hours of work each week.[^7] That second rule is a workforce ratio test, not an individual entitlement, and it is measured across a placed workforce. The [triangular workforce ratio note]({{< ref "/blog/aewv-triangular-nz-workforce-ratio" >}}) covers it.

An employer who reports against the ratio and assumes the individual floor is covered has answered the wrong question. They are separate evidence categories in INZ's own post-accreditation check list.[^7]

## What to do

1. Pull the last 26 weeks of timesheet or payroll data for every AEWV worker and find any week below 30 paid hours. That is your exposure list, and it exists whether or not anyone has raised it.
2. Check each AEWV employment agreement against the guaranteed hours approved in the matching Job Check. The agreement figure cannot be lower.[^4]
3. Put a payroll-level rule in place, not a manager-level one. A roster review catches the pattern; a payroll exception report catches the week.
4. Decide in advance what happens in a genuine downturn. Topping up to 30 hours is compliant. Reducing hours is not, and unpaid leave in a quiet period will read the same way in a timesheet.
5. If a worker asks to reduce their hours, treat it as an immigration question before an HR one. The visa was granted on the hours in the Job Check, and conditions are varied by application, not by agreement between you and the worker.[^3]
6. Where a role's hours or location genuinely change, check whether a new Job Check is required before the change takes effect rather than after.

*AEWV Compliance is scoping a tool in this area — surfacing the weeks where guaranteed hours were not met is one of the checks we are looking at.*

## What we don't know

INZ does not publish how the 30-hour condition is assessed across a period. Instructions say "every week worked," which reads as a weekly test rather than an average.[^1] But the published guidance does not address partial weeks at the start and end of employment, weeks containing public holidays, or weeks where a worker takes approved unpaid leave at their own request. We have not found a source that resolves any of those, and the difference matters — a weekly test with no tolerance is a much sharper rule than most employers are administering.

Nor does INZ publish how often accreditation is revoked or suspended specifically for under-provision of guaranteed hours, as distinct from other breaches. The revocation power is stated plainly.[^2] [^6] The frequency of its use here is not.
