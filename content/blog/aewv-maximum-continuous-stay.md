Every AEWV worker runs on two clocks. The first is visa expiry — printed on the visa, widely tracked. The second is the AEWV maximum continuous stay: the total time a worker can spend in New Zealand across one or more AEWVs before they must leave for 12 months.[^1] The second clock appears on no document the employer holds, keeps running across renewals, and cannot be extended by goodwill on either side. INZ's own employer guidance, updated 11 June 2026, now tells employers to monitor it and to start conversations early.[^2]

## Why this matters now

More than 94,000 people currently hold an AEWV.[^2] The first waves are reaching their limits: INZ ran a bridging arrangement in late 2025 for workers who would hit their maximum continuous stay by 30 November 2025, letting time on certain Specific Purpose Work Visas count toward the total — a sign the timing-out cohort is no longer hypothetical.[^1] Workers hired into lower-skilled roles after the April 2024 rule change, which cut their maximum stay from five years to three, begin reaching their limits from 2027 — inside the planning horizon of any employer deciding now whether to invest in their training.[^2]

## Maximum continuous stay is the second clock

A visa expiry date tells you when the current visa ends. The maximum continuous stay tells you how much total AEWV time the worker has. If the current visa is shorter than the maximum, the worker can apply for further AEWVs without leaving the country.[^1] When the total is reached, no further regular AEWV is available until the worker has spent 12 months in a row outside New Zealand.[^3]

The maximum is five years for most roles: ANZSCO or NOL skill levels 1 to 3, Green List jobs, and roles paid at or above $52.50 per hour all qualify.[^1] For ANZSCO skill level 4 and 5 roles outside those categories, the maximum is three years.[^1] There are sector carve-outs — care workforce jobs eligible for the Care Workforce Work to Residence Visa qualify for five years when paid at least $28.25 per hour, as do approved transport roles paid at least $35.00[^1] — and a transitional rule: workers who applied between 21 June 2023 and 6 April 2024 and were paid at least $29.66 per hour at grant also hold a five-year maximum.[^1]

That list is why the second clock is invisible in most HR systems: the answer depends on skill level, pay rate, occupation list status, and when the first AEWV was applied for. Two workers hired the same week can hold different maximums.

## How INZ counts the clock

The count starts when the worker got their first AEWV if they were already in New Zealand, or when they arrived on it if they applied from offshore.[^1] From there, the counting rules cut against casual assumptions:

- **Interim visas between AEWVs count.** A worker waiting on an Interim Visa for their next AEWV to be approved is spending maximum-stay time, even though they hold no AEWV during the wait.[^1]
- **Other visas do not count.** Time on student, visitor, Post Study Work, partnership, or working holiday visas sits outside the total. A worker who moved from a Post Study Work Visa to an AEWV starts the clock at the AEWV, not at arrival.[^1]
- **A narrow SPWV exception exists.** Time on a Specific Purpose Work Visa counts only for workers in the late-2025 bridging window — applied between 8 September and 2 November 2025, in one of 30 named NOL occupations, reaching their maximum by 30 November 2025.[^1]

An employer estimating a worker's remaining runway from hire date alone can be wrong in both directions.

## What happens at the limit — and the one lever that moves it

At the end of the maximum continuous stay, the worker must spend at least 12 months in a row outside New Zealand before another regular AEWV can be granted.[^3] For the employer, that is a 12-month loss of a trained worker, with no discretion in the rule. INZ's employer guidance is blunt about the compliance edge: a worker who has reached their maximum stay cannot work lawfully, and employing someone without a valid visa can affect the employer's accreditation.[^2]

One lever genuinely moves the clock: skill level. A worker on the three-year maximum who is offered a higher-skilled job that carries a five-year total can stay five years instead of three — and a worker who has already left can return before completing the 12 months outside.[^1] Pay is part of that lever: $52.50 per hour buys the five-year maximum regardless of skill level.[^1] The [high-paying thresholds article]({{< ref "/blog/aewv-high-paying-wage-thresholds" >}}) covers when that arithmetic makes sense. Residence pathways are the other exit — INZ itself points employers toward supporting workers into residence options where available.[^2]

## Renewals inside the limit are not automatic

A worker with unused maximum stay still needs a fresh application for each further AEWV — and the mechanics tightened in 2025. For AEWVs applied for on or after 10 March 2025, job tokens cannot be reused: every renewal requires a new approved Job Check, with whatever advertising or Work and Income engagement that entails.[^3] Only workers who got their current AEWV on or before 9 March 2025, in the same role, employer, and location, at the same or higher pay, can renew on a reused token.[^3]

Renewals also meet the current rule set, not the one in force at first hire — including the English language requirement that reached skill level 3 roles on 1 June 2026.[^3] For employers, that means the renewal lead time now includes a possible Job Check and possible English evidence, planned back from the visa expiry date — while the maximum-stay date determines whether the renewal is worth running at all. The [visa expiry records article]({{< ref "/blog/aewv-visa-expiry-reminder-records" >}}) covers the reminder obligations that sit alongside this.

*AEWV Compliance is scoping a tool in this area — a per-worker view that holds visa expiry, maximum-stay date, and renewal lead time together is one of the concepts we are testing.*

## What to do

1. For each AEWV worker, record two dates: visa expiry and estimated maximum continuous stay. Build the second from first-AEWV grant or arrival date, skill level, and pay rate — and check it against INZ's online stay tool rather than assumption.[^1]
2. Flag every worker on a three-year maximum. For each, decide the path 12 months out: a higher-skilled role or pay change that lifts the maximum to five years, a residence pathway, or a planned departure with a documented handover.[^1][^2]
3. Audit interim visa periods in each worker's history — they count toward the total and are the most common source of overestimated runway.[^1]
4. For renewals, plan backward from expiry: a new Job Check is required for anyone whose current AEWV was applied for on or after 10 March 2025, and skill level 3 renewals may need English evidence.[^3]
5. Put maximum-stay status on the agenda of any workforce planning that assumes an AEWV worker is still present in two years.

## What we don't know

INZ says the required time outside New Zealand is "normally 12 months in a row" — what circumstances, if any, qualify for exceptions is not enumerated in the public guidance.[^3] Nor is it stated how short return visits during the 12 months are treated for regular AEWV holders. And the stay calculation for workers with complex visa histories — multiple interim periods, Job Changes, offshore gaps — ultimately depends on INZ's records, not the employer's; the online tool helps, but it is not a determination. Where the date matters, verify it before relying on it.
